Macavation · Sales & Production Reporting · Version 2

Reports on WhatsApp

Tap a button, get an answer. What the daily, weekly and monthly reports will look like — and what people can do with them without typing a word.

Version 1 of this document had people typing words like WEEK to get a figure. That was the wrong design.

We already have a WhatsApp product — Howdie — that solved these problems properly, with tested code we can reuse. This version uses that approach: tappable buttons and menus, not typed commands. It also fixes a real problem that version 1 missed about the weekly and monthly reports.

Section 1

What changed, and why

I went and read how Howdie handles WhatsApp. Three things came out of it.

1. The WhatsApp rule I described is real — but there is a far better way to live with it

WhatsApp genuinely will not let a business send an unprompted message in free wording. Howdie's own notes say exactly the same thing. That part of version 1 was correct and does not change.

What version 1 got wrong was the consequence. I assumed a fixed-format message was a dead end — that people would have to type words back to get anything more. It is not. A fixed-format message can carry buttons. The moment somebody taps one, the conversation is open and we can reply with anything we like, in whatever wording suits.

Version 1

Type WEEK to get week-to-date figures. Remember the right word, spell it correctly, and hope.

Version 2

Tap Week to date. A button on the message itself. Nothing to remember, nothing to spell, nothing to teach anybody.

2. Version 1 missed a problem with the weekly and monthly reports

I applied the WhatsApp rule to the daily report and stopped there. It applies to the weekly and monthly reports too. Pete's send works today because he sends into conversations that are already live — but a shareholder who has not messaged Macavation in three weeks would simply not receive the report, and nobody would find out why.

Section 5 shows how that gets handled.

3. We are not writing this from scratch

Howdie already has a tested library for sending WhatsApp buttons, menus and fixed-format messages — written against the live messaging system rather than guessed at. Macavation reuses it as-is. That takes real risk and real time out of the build.

Also already done

Macavation's WhatsApp channel is active, and the security key needed to register the fixed-format messages was already created on 30 July and has never been used. So there is less setup ahead of us than version 1 assumed.

The three buttons are the whole difference

They are part of the message. Nobody has to know a command exists, and nothing depends on spelling. Tapping one sends a reply back to us, which is what lets us answer in full sentences with whatever detail is useful.

WhatsApp allows three buttons on a message like this, each label up to twenty characters. More options is the third one because it opens a longer menu — that is how we get past the limit of three.

What happens at five o'clock

The system pulls the latest figures off the factory system first, then reads them back and sends. The numbers are as fresh as the factory data, not a snapshot from whenever somebody last pressed refresh in the portal.

If Pete has corrected a figure

His correction wins. The refresh only updates the raw factory mirror — it never overwrites a number a person deliberately entered.

On a day with no production

Nothing is sent. No Sunday message, no public holiday message, no "0 kg".

You will not get it twice

The system checks whether today has already gone out before sending again.

The one thing to decide

Four figures plus week to date — cracked, kernel packed, wholes percentage, NIS received. And three buttons — Week to date, Full breakdown, More options.

Both the figures and the button labels have to be registered with WhatsApp before the first message can go out. Changing them later means submitting again and waiting for approval a second time, so this is the moment to add, drop or rename anything.

Each answer carries its own buttons

Nothing can be changed from a phone

Every button only reads information. There is no way to enter production figures, edit a report, or send anything to anybody else from a handset. That is a deliberate limit — a phone is too easy to pick up.

Only known staff get figures

The system already knows which phone numbers belong to which staff member. Production figures only go to a number it recognises. A shareholder who taps Full breakdown gets a polite "not available on this number", not the tonnage.

The buttons keep going

Somebody can move from the day, to the week, to stock on hand without ever typing or opening the portal.

Typing still works

If somebody types "week" or "figures" anyway, it is understood. The buttons exist so nobody has to — not to stop anyone who prefers typing.

Everything is recorded

Who asked, what they asked, and what they were told, including anything refused. Already built.

Stopping is on the menu, not hidden

Stop daily messages and Pause for 7 days sit in the same list as everything else. This matters more than it looks: if somebody cannot easily make the messages stop, they block the number — and then they never receive the monthly report either.

Pause is usually the right one

Somebody going on leave taps Pause for 7 days and it resumes on its own. Nobody has to remember to put them back.

Stopping does not delete them

Their row stays on Pete's list, marked as stopped, so he can see it happened rather than wondering where they went.

Anyone can stop, only staff get figures

Stopping and the menu work for everybody on the list. The production figures behind the menu are still staff-only.

Menu labels are short by rule

WhatsApp caps each line at 24 characters, which is why they read as Kernel stock on hand rather than anything longer. Ten lines is the hard maximum, so nine leaves one spare.

Section 5

The weekly and monthly reports

Pete still sends these himself, by hand, after he has checked and edited them. That does not change. What changes is that they will now reach people who have not been chatting to us.

The problem version 1 missed. The same WhatsApp rule applies here. Today Pete's send only works for someone who has messaged Macavation in the last day. A shareholder who has not would silently not receive the report. The fix is to send whichever version WhatsApp allows, and the system works that out per person — Pete does nothing differently.

Fixed format — the buttons do the rest

Tapping "Read summary" opens the conversation, so Pete's full note arrives as an ordinary message straight afterwards. The recipient sees the same words either way. They just get there in one extra tap.

A security improvement that comes with this

The Open report button needs a short, tidy web address, so the 30-day link that currently sits in the chat is replaced by a short code we look up when it is tapped. The actual file link is then created fresh at the moment of opening and expires within minutes — instead of a working link to a confidential report sitting in a WhatsApp chat for a month.

It also means a link can be switched off if a report is withdrawn. Today it cannot be.

The Staff tick is what decides whether the menu will hand somebody production figures. A shareholder gets the reports they are ticked for and can stop them, but tapping Today's production politely declines.

Two protections built in

  • The same person cannot be added twice. Saving someone as 082 555 0110 and again as +27 82 555 0110 is caught as the same number. Nobody gets a confidential report twice, and nobody is missed because they were on the list under a different format.
  • Stopping does not delete them. The row stays, marked stopped, so Pete can see it happened.
Needed from Macavation

The real list: names, WhatsApp numbers, which of the three reports each person gets, and who counts as staff. Nothing can be properly tested against made-up numbers.

What it costs to send

WhatsApp charges per conversation for an unprompted message like the daily. A tapped button opens a conversation that lasts 24 hours, so somebody tapping five buttons costs no more than tapping one. For a handful of recipients once a day it is small money, but it scales with the number of people on the daily list — worth knowing before that list reaches thirty.
