First, the rule everything else hangs off

I told you earlier that reports can't be pushed because the member has to open the chat within 24 hours. That was wrong, and it changes the design — so it goes first.

Can be sent cold, any time
  • An approved template — a fixed wording Meta has signed off, with your figures dropped into the blanks.
  • Up to 3 quick-reply buttons attached to it.
  • A document, image or header carried by the template.
No reply needed. The member may never have messaged you. This is how EggSight has been sending daily farm reports for years.
Needs an open window
  • Free-typed text — anything you want to say, no approval.
  • Interactive menus — the tappable list of options.
  • PDFs and files sent on their own.
  • Forms with dropdowns and date pickers.
The window opens when they act, lasts 24 hours, and everything sent inside it is free.
The hinge A quick-reply button on a template is the cheapest way across. One tap counts as them replying — so the tap delivers the report and opens the window for everything that follows. Push and pull stop being a choice.

One template per frequency, acting as the notification rather than the report itself:

Plus macavation_weekly_report and macavation_monthly_report. That is the complete set.
Why not copy EggSight. EggSight bakes the farm name into the template text and cycles a different template for each day of the week — so it needs seven approved templates per report type per farm, thirty-one in monthly mode, plus a hardcoded patch for the days Meta rejected. It works, but it is a lot of moving parts to keep approved. Putting the name and date in the blanks instead gets the same result from three templates.
Worth knowing before we commit. Templates take up to 24 hours for Meta to approve, and wording changes mean re-approval. Right now the channel has no approved template at all — the one that exists was never submitted — so nothing can currently be sent unprompted, including the enrolment code. Getting these three approved is the long pole.

What changes

  • Any unrecognised word opens the menu. Hello, Hi and Start already do. But stock please gets a list of verbs to type instead of the menu — which is the moment people give up.
  • A second level. The menu is currently flat — one tap, one answer. Reports and settings want a sub-menu.
  • Typed numbers keep working. They match the position in the same list, so the number is always right whether or not the sheet rendered.
Already true. The list, the numbered fallback, position-matching, and each item being hidden unless your role can see that area in the portal — all built and live. So is the confirm-before-acting pattern for anything that changes data.
Two keys never change. 0 is back and 99 is main menu, on every screen, across every Customapp WhatsApp product. Worth keeping even where a button would do, so people who learn it once are never wrong.

The risk worth naming. Meta scores your number on how people react to it. Too many messages nobody wanted and the score drops — and a dropped score cuts how many messages you may send of any kind, including the reports. So alert noise doesn't just annoy people, it degrades the reports. Hence the severity floor, the dedupe, and a cap on how many alerts one person can get in a day.
Mostly built already. The rules, the alert table, the dedupe, the portal bell, and the ask-then-confirm-then-resolve flow all exist. What's missing is the branch that sends it to a handset.

Cheaper than it looks. The pause date already has a column, and the sender already respects it. The function that changes a subscription from a phone number is already written and granted — it has simply never been called by anything. This is mostly wiring.

Two rules I'd hold to

  • Pause beats unsubscribe. Someone on leave wants quiet for a week, not to be forgotten. A pause that expires on its own means nobody has to remember to switch them back on.
  • Stop always works, immediately, whatever the message. Meta requires it and so does POPIA. It should never take more than one message, and it should never be buried behind a menu.
Adding yourself. Your panel already tells people they can join by messaging the number. Making that true is a small job and closes the loop — someone new asks the number for the daily report, confirms, and appears in your list without you touching anything.

Four things I'd add that you didn't ask for

All of them small, and all of them things you'll want the first week it's live.

Did they read it?

WhatsApp tells us when a message is delivered and when it's read, and we already record it. Nothing shows it. A ✓✓ read 07:12 next to each name answers the question you'd otherwise ask by phone.

Send it to yourself first

One button that sends the real report to your own handset. Catches a wrong figure or an awkward sentence before it reaches the shareholders.

What it's costing

Every unprompted message costs a fraction of a rand; every reply inside a window is free. A running count of both, on the same screen, keeps the bill from being a surprise — and shows the design is working, because the free half should be the larger one.

Short links instead of long ones

Report links currently go out as a long signed web address valid for 30 days. A short /r/… link that expires in minutes is already built and deployed — it just isn't being used yet. Tidier to read and safer if forwarded.

A weekly and monthly sender

Those two ticks on your panel currently do nothing at all, because there's no sender behind them. Worth building alongside the daily rather than after it — same shape, same template pattern.

Point the number at live data

The live WhatsApp number currently answers from the development database. Anyone testing it today gets development figures. Worth moving deliberately, before real people start trusting the answers.

One live fault worth fixing whatever we decide here. Every Menu you send comes back saying your role has access to nothing — while your role in fact has 32 areas enabled. The permission lookup hides a super user's own permissions unless the thing asking is itself signed in as a super user, and the bot isn't signed in as anyone. Every other role returns correctly. You happen to hold the one role it breaks on, so it looks completely broken while working fine for everybody else. It's a small fix.
Suggested order. Submit the three templates first — the approval clock is the long pole and everything unprompted waits on it. Fix the permission fault the same day so you can actually use the menu while we wait. Then deploy and schedule the daily, add the weekly and monthly, and wire up the self-service settings. Alerts and receipts after that. Forms last, or not at all for now.

Stock and batch figures shown in the menu screens are real values read from the development database on 4 September 2026.
