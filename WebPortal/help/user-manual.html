Introduction

Macavation is a web application for tracking macadamia material from intake through production, quality checks, warehouse stock, and dispatch. Two product streams run in parallel:

Kernel stream

  1. Grower Intake (wet NIS, receiving, samples)
  2. Kernel Production (job cards, stages)
  3. Stock (Kernel) and Kernel Dispatch

Oil and protein stream

  1. Supplier Intake (oil-side receiving and batches)
  2. Oil Production (sheets, finished goods)
  3. Stock (Oil and Protein) and Oil Dispatch

CRM, Quality Assurance, documents, forecasting, finance views, and ERP integration support both streams. What you see in the menu depends on your role; some items are hidden until an administrator grants access.

Scope of this manual

This document is built from the Web Portal as shipped in the repo: sidebar structure in index.html, registered routes in js/appRouteConfig.json, and the contextual topics in help/index.html. It is meant for end users and administrators of the UI.

What this manual does not replace. It does not document Supabase schemas, Row Level Security, Lambda proxy APIs, Palladium field mappings, deployment environments, or custom client branding. Those belong in technical runbooks or vendor documentation. It also does not walk every dialog line-by-line—that detail is in the in-app guide’s per-screen topics.

Overlay routes: Many *-modal entries in the route config load as dialogs on top of a grid. They usually have no sidebar link; you open them from buttons or row actions inside the owning module.

Sign-in and access

You sign in through the portal sign-in page. Your organisation may give you a link that includes a client code (cc in the URL). Use the credentials issued by your administrator.

Session and security. Do not share accounts. If you forget your password, ask an administrator to reset it. Sign out or lock your device when you leave a shared PC.

After sign-in, the app loads your last page or the default dashboard. If you are sent back to sign-in, your session expired or you are not authenticated.

Navigation and roles

The sidebar lists modules your role may open. Click a link to load that module in the main content area. Some modules offer Board, Table, or other view toggles; pick the view your team prefers.

In table views, the last column is usually Actions: click the green ⋯ button on a row to open edit, view, delete, or other steps for that record.

Access denied

If you open a route you are not allowed to use, you may see a warning and a short redirect to the dashboard. Ask an administrator to check your role, Role features, and Role permissions if you need a module for your job.

User & access (restricted)

User & access (admin-grid) is the normal home for accounts, roles, which portal modules each role sees, and (for power users) database function permissions. A separate Features catalogue may appear in the same sidebar group when your role includes it. Legacy standalone grids (users-grid, roles-grid, and others) can remain available as routes for bookmarks even when the sidebar shows only the hub.

Application shell (global UI)

Beyond individual modules, the portal provides shared controls:

  • Sign-in (signin.html) — authentication and optional client code in the URL; successful login loads index.html.
  • Top navbar — user menu, profile access, and Sign out.
  • Sidebar — journey-centric groups: Home (Dashboard, My Day), Find a batch, Kernel pipeline and Oil pipeline (four numbered steps each), and Support (forecasts, CRM, Quality, Documents, Business, integrations, User & access).
  • My Day — in the sidebar under Home and in the header dropdown; shows intake, production, and stock work queues with Do now buttons.
  • Sidebar footer — Dark mode toggle and Collapse to widen the content area.

The main content area loads HTML/JS per route; breadcrumbs and last page may be restored from storage after refresh.

End-to-end process

Before working in a single module, it helps to know where a batch “lives” in the flow:

  1. Intake records the delivery and initial checks (grower or supplier).
  2. Production transforms material; job cards and production sheets document the run.
  3. Quality Assurance may hold or release material based on tests and your rules.
  4. Stock reflects warehouse location, status, and quantities available for dispatch.
  5. Dispatch builds baskets, runs inspection paperwork, and completes shipment.

Dashboard

dashboard

The dashboard is the default landing area for many users. It can include multiple views depending on configuration. On overview and executive views, major panels load collapsed—expand a section heading when you need that detail.

  • Dashboard overview — operational snapshot: production, packing, tasks, and links into deeper grids.
  • Material journey — integrator-style view of movement and alerts; use Refresh before acting on cards.
  • Executive dashboard — KPI-style widgets and reporting; use Customize and export options where available. On Production Trends, the Hide weekends switch (on by default in daily/Monthly view) removes Saturday and Sunday from the chart so weekday production connects without closure gaps; it is disabled in Yearly view. Stock on hand history shows a multi-line chart of kg on hand per kernel style or oil stream over time, with Kernel/Oil toggle and 1M–All range buttons.

Typical tasks

  1. Open the dashboard from the sidebar after sign-in.
  2. Expand the sections you need using the chevron on each panel heading.
  3. Read summary cards for the day; drill into linked modules if a number needs investigation.
  4. If figures look wrong, refresh the browser once before assuming a data fault.

Data audit panel (administrators)

The Data audit panel appears on the executive dashboard for users with administrative permissions. It shows the raw source counts that sit behind dashboard numbers, grouped by their data source (kernel, oil, and similar). Use this panel to verify that dashboard figures reflect the underlying data correctly, particularly after importing historical records or when a displayed number appears incorrect.

The panel loads only when first expanded and includes a Refresh link to re-run the audit query. Each metric shows its human-readable label with the raw database key in a tooltip, so you can trace it back to the source function. If detailed breakdowns are available (for example, counts by status), they appear as formatted JSON beneath the metric.

My Day

my-day

My Day is your start-of-shift view: work queues for intake, production, and stock/dispatch (with Do now buttons), plus role-based tasks from the server when configured.

  1. Open My Day from the sidebar under Home (or the header dropdown).
  2. Work items in an order your team agrees (priority, age, or customer urgency).
  3. Follow each item into the owning module; completing work there updates queues and dashboards for others.

Find a batch

batch-journey

Use Find a batch when you know a batch or grower but not which module currently owns the work. Search kernel batches across the full journey, or switch to the Oil & Protein tab for supplier oil batches. Each row shows one status label and an Open button that takes you to the correct module (Grower Intake, Kernel Production, Stock, and so on).

  1. Search by batch, grower, or other supported fields.
  2. Filter and sort (for example by status or date) to shorten the list.
  3. Click a row to open batch history. The Dispatch tab lists kernel dispatch orders that include this batch (buyer, dates, style, cartons, and kg). Use Intake, Production, and End sample (QA) for the other timeline panes (empty tabs are hidden). Use Edit → Archive to send a batch to the historical archive and remove it from active lists. Restore from Stock → View archive when needed.

CRM — Contact database

crm-grid

CRM holds contacts used across the system: growers (NIS suppliers), kernel customers, oil and protein customers, ingredient suppliers, and oil processors. Accurate CRM data reduces errors on dispatch documents and reports.

Tabs (typical)

  • NIS suppliers — numbered grower or supplier list for kernel intake and related reporting.
  • Kernel customers — buyers for kernel dispatch.
  • Oil and protein customers — buyers for oil dispatch and sales.
  • Oil ingredient suppliers and Oil processors — upstream oil stream contacts.

Typical tasks

  1. Open CRM from the sidebar. For NIS and legacy supplier contact types, maintain the two-digit supplier code on each record so kernel batch suggestions use the correct SS segment.
  2. Select the tab that matches the contact type you need.
  3. Use filters and search; add or edit contacts, or import from Excel using the approved template (do not change column headers).

Grower Intake

grower-intake-grid

Grower Intake is the kernel-side receiving process: it includes a Procurement Calendar for planning expected deliveries, as well as receiving checklists, batch testing and samples, and creation of kernel batches before production.

Procurement Calendar

The collapsible Procurement Calendar sits above the search bar (it loads collapsed—expand the heading when scheduling). It lets the team schedule expected grower deliveries before they arrive, so purchasing intentions are visible to everyone in the portal.

  • Click any calendar day to select it. The right panel shows existing procurement entries for that day and an Add procurement form.
  • Fill in the optional grower/supplier dropdown, an optional free-text grower name override, and the predicted weight in kg, then click Add procurement. The delivery appears as a pill on the day.
  • Drag a pill to a different day to reschedule it. The date is updated immediately.
  • When the delivery physically arrives, drag the pill from the calendar into the Receiving column of the Kanban board below. The Create kernel batch dialog opens pre-filled with the grower, scheduled date, and predicted weight. Adjust if needed and save — the batch is created in Receiving and the procurement entry is marked as converted and removed from the calendar.
  • Click the × button next to any entry in the day panel to delete a scheduled delivery.
  • Navigate between months using the < / > arrows in the calendar header. The calendar collapses with the chevron button.

Batch intake workflow

  • Receiving checklist (stage 1) must be completed before batch test and sample actions unlock.
  • Create kernel batch registers a new intake record tied to a CRM grower/supplier. Each batch number must be unique among active kernel batches — you cannot create a second active batch with the same number while one is still in use.
  • Release to production hands the batch to Kernel Production when quality and checklist rules are satisfied.
  • Archive batch (row action) sends the batch to the historical archive and removes it from active intake/production lists. Restore from Stock → View archive when needed.

Typical tasks

  1. Open Grower Intake; use Board or Table view as preferred.
  2. Add expected deliveries on the Procurement Calendar so the team knows what is coming and when.
  3. When a delivery arrives, drag its calendar pill into the Receiving lane (or use Create kernel batch manually).
  4. Complete the receiving checklist, then batch test and sample steps.
  5. Release to production when all quality steps are satisfied.

Grower Intake · Receiving checklist · Create kernel batch · Link sample to batch

Kernel Production

kernel-production-grid

Kernel Production tracks batches through factory steps: job cards, production stages, batch summary, history, and hand-off toward stock. Use Production Calendar from the toolbar when you need day-by-day activity.

Typical tasks

  1. Open Kernel Production; open Production Calendar if needed, then review filters.
  2. Open a batch card or row to manage job cards and production stages in order.
  3. Use Batch summary, Batch history, or view-only job card dialogs for audits and customer queries.
  4. Review the job card (production can prefill style lines). Press Save job card to keep edits; press Jobcard approved to commit stock quantities.
  5. Complete release to stock when the batch is release ready and the job card is approved.
  6. Archive batch (row menu) sends the batch to the historical archive; restore from Stock → View archive when needed.

Stock (Kernel)

stock-management-kernel

Stock (Kernel) shows kernel warehouse positions and movements. Per-style on hand comes from the job card after Jobcard approved (and release to stock), not from production packing sheets alone. Finished kernel stock that is eligible for sale is associated with the kernel finished location (your organisation may refer to this as KERNEL R YES versus raw NIS).

Typical tasks

  1. Open Stock (Kernel) from the sidebar.
  2. Filter by status, product type, and location; confirm the on-screen list matches the physical warehouse.
  3. Use Send to Dispatch when moving selected lines toward a kernel dispatch basket (quantities must match reality).
  4. On Kernel stock by style, use Edit to update batch details (number, grower, received date, wet NIS, FFA, best before) when your role allows. Turn on Adjust Stock to capture cartons or kg (the system calculates boxes before saving). A batch appears on By style only when at least one style still has remaining boxes (cartons or kg equivalent). FFA or historical production yield alone does not keep a batch on the grid — including in Adjust Stock. To restore or correct a batch with no remaining stock, use Add Batch, search by batch number, and click a match to bring it back. You can also fill supplier and styles there to create a new historical batch. Use Archive (batch row action) to send a batch to the historical archive — it records who archived it and when. Open View archive (toolbar) to search archived batches, see who archived them, and Restore. If the original batch number is already in use by another active batch, enter a new number when prompted. Batch numbers may be reused after archive, but not while another active batch holds the number. Use Send back to production when the batch should return to the production flow instead.
  5. Use stock take, receiving checklist, or raw material issued dialogs when your process requires adjustments or issues to production.
Historical import. Use any "import historical" function only when administrators have approved a controlled load. It is not a substitute for normal intake and production postings.

Shell waste movement history

Shell waste lots track by-products from kernel production. Each lot records creation, adjustments, dispatch, and write-offs over its lifetime. Use Movement history from the shell lot row menu to see all movements for a lot, including when each movement occurred, the type of movement, quantity in kg, reference, and any notes. Movement history is available to all users with access to stock management.

Kernel Dispatch

kernel-dispatch-grid

Kernel Dispatch manages despatch baskets for kernel customers: inspection, dispatch paperwork, and basket lines. Baskets are built from stock movements and CRM kernel customers.

  1. Open Kernel Dispatch; use Board or Table as preferred.
  2. Find a basket by typing in Search (buyer, linked CRM contact, grower on any line, or batch; a single letter uses starts-with on batches and start-of-word on names). Optionally set Supplier received. With both set, only baskets matching both are listed. Use Clear to show all baskets again.
  3. Review open baskets; open View sheet to see basket lines together with the full inspection and dispatch form (driver, vehicle checks, transport, signatures).
  4. Complete Dispatch and the same form for orders not yet dispatched.
  5. Dispatched orders open the form read-only so you can review what was recorded. Use Edit on the sheet or on a dispatched grid row only when dispatch was recorded by mistake; saved paperwork is cleared and the basket returns to ready to dispatch so you can change lines or dispatch again.
  6. Review dispatched baskets for completed paperwork.

If quantities are wrong, correct stock or the basket in Stock Management rather than dispatching incorrect loads.

Kernel Dispatch · Dispatch modal · Dispatch form

Supplier Intake

supplier-intake-grid

Supplier Intake covers oil and protein raw material arriving from suppliers: receiver checklists create batches; oil batch headers and tests move material toward oil production.

  1. Open Supplier Intake; choose Board, Table, Weekly, or Overview as needed.
  2. Start a new delivery with Receiver checklist; tie the supplier to CRM and capture manufactured date per bag.
  3. Best before date is auto-calculated as manufactured date + 18 months in the receiver checklist flow.
  4. In both Receiver checklist and Quick stock entry, Add bag asks how many rows to add, copies first-row values, and sequences batch numbers automatically.
  5. Quick stock entry follows the same rule: enter manufactured date per bag, with best-before calculated automatically (+18 months).
  6. Complete sample tests when enabled; release batches toward oil production when rules pass.
  7. Edit batch headers through row actions only where your role allows post-receipt changes.

oil-production-grid

Oil Production manages extraction and finishing steps for the oil stream: raw batches released from supplier intake, production sheets (food-grade oil, protein powder, cosmetic oil, and similar), and links to finished stock. Stock (Oil and Protein)

stock-management-oil

Document Management

document-management-grid

File Explorer-style browser for operational documents. Folders are nested (any depth); files live inside folders or at the root. Folder structure is preserved when uploading an entire directory from your computer.

  1. Open Document Management. The root view shows top-level folders and loose files.
  2. Click a folder name to navigate into it; use the Back button or breadcrumbs to go up.
  3. Click New folder to create a subfolder in the current location (Home or whichever folder you are viewing).
  4. Click Upload while inside any folder to add files there. Use Select files for one or many files, or Upload folder to upload a whole directory — its subfolder tree is recreated automatically.
  5. Each file must be 6 MB or smaller; oversized files are flagged in the upload preview.
  6. For a single file, enter a custom title (filename is pre-filled). For multiple files or a folder upload, titles come from filenames automatically.
  7. A progress line tracks each file; a summary on completion lists successes and any failures.
  8. Use the Search box to find files and folders across all levels; results include the folder path.
  9. Delete a file with its row action. Deleting a folder removes all nested content after confirmation.

User & access

admin-grid

User & access is the unified administration hub: People (user list and edit with optional password), Roles & modules (full-width roles list; Customize opens module toggles plus per-module button actions and database permissions, Edit role edits name and description, Deactivate retires a role when the admin.roles.delete action is granted), and System (placeholder or environment tools depending on deployment).

Typical tasks

  1. Open User & access from the sidebar.
  2. On People, add or edit users; assign roles; use the same edit dialog behaviour as the standalone Users screen where applicable.
  3. On Roles & modules, use Customize to align module toggles with responsibility, expand a module there to adjust its database function permissions, and use Delete role when your role has admin.users.manage or admin.roles.delete. The super_user role is visible here but only a super_user actor may edit or customize it; the same role is hidden from other permission admin screens.
  4. super_user accounts and @customapp.co.za staff logins are visible only to other super_user actors in the People list.
  5. After permission or feature changes, test with a non-admin login; users may need to sign out and back in to refresh menu visibility.

Troubleshooting Customize

  • Module grants are per role — changing Production Manager does not automatically change Admin or other roles.
  • Wait for Customize to finish loading before toggling checkboxes.
  • In Customize, Module names match sidebar labels and appear in sidebar order; Key is the technical route id.
  • If your own sidebar loses User & access while you edit another role, sign out and back in; your role’s grants may still be correct in the database.

Standalone user routes (bookmarks)

The app still registers these routes for deep links and training materials. When your role includes the admin-grid feature, the sidebar normally hides duplicate entries for the four legacy keys and shows the hub instead. Button actions are configured only in the hub (Roles & modules → Customize → expand module → Button actions).

  • Users (users-grid) — standalone user grid (same edit patterns as People in the hub when both exist).
  • Roles (roles-grid) — standalone roles grid.
  • Database Role Permissions (role-permissions-grid) — full matrix; the hub now manages the same permissions per module under Roles & modules → Customize.
  • Role Features (role-features-grid) — legacy screen; hub Roles & modules → Customize replaces it for daily work.
  • Features (features-grid) — catalogue of feature keys; remains a sibling item under User & access when assigned.
After permission changes, test with a non-admin user. Features and permissions work together with menu visibility; fixing “cannot see menu X” often requires more than one control.
